\section{Introduction}

World models, which capture the structure and dynamics of the physical
world, are emerging as a foundation for agents, robotics, and physical
AI. Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) are a compelling
direction: they predict masked or future content directly in embedding
space, avoiding costly pixel generation while promoting semantic
abstraction. Yet the settings on which such models are evaluated are
overwhelmingly lower-density and lane-structured, far from the
environments where physical AI must ultimately operate.

We study a largely unexplored regime: populous, crowded, and chaotic
Global South city streets, which we call \textbf{DENSEWORLD}
(Figure~\ref{fig:denseworld_scene_types}). Here, soft spatial
boundaries, extreme agent heterogeneity, persistent occlusion, and rapid
social negotiation under mixed traffic replace the lane-structured
assumptions behind existing benchmarks. These are precisely the
properties a world model must respect to forecast how a scene will
evolve, and precisely the ones that off-the-shelf encoders are not
trained to preserve.

Frozen JEPA encoders struggle in this regime: they fail to preserve
dense interaction dynamics under heterogeneity and partial
observability, and generic fine-tuning closes only part of the gap. The
difficulty is structural rather than a matter of scale. The future of a
crowded street is governed by \emph{who} is where (agents), \emph{what}
the scene affords (layout), and \emph{how} agents negotiate
(interactions); a monolithic latent blurs these together and lets the
predictor take cross-factor shortcuts.

Our central idea is to make world structure a \emph{first-class
predictive primitive}. Rather than adapting a JEPA predictor on
undifferentiated video, \textbf{\emph{FactorJEPA}} performs
\emph{predictor surgery}: a three-stage curriculum over automatically
separated \emph{layout}, \emph{agent}, and \emph{interaction} views,
with a visibility gate that preserves partially observed agents and
separated subspaces that discourage cross-factor shortcuts.
Figure~\ref{fig:demo_cards} illustrates the payoff on held-out clips:
the same probe question is answered more accurately by our factor-view
predictor surgery than by the frozen backbone, with only the encoder
changed.

We make three contributions. First, we release \textbf{DENSEWORLD 1.0}:
115,687 videos (276 hours) across 22 Indian cities, 86,831 of them with
automatically derived layout, agent, and interaction targets. Second, we
introduce \textbf{FactorJEPA} and predictor surgery, which turn
factorized world structure into an explicit training signal for the
predictor rather than a monolithic fine-tuning objective. Third, on a
15-diagnostic evaluation harness, FactorJEPA separates from all six
fine-tuning baselines (24 of 24 paired wins) and its rankings replicate
across backbone scale ($\rho=0.895$ to $0.978$), making the 1B model a
half-cost proxy for 2B.

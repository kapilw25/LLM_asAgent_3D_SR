\begin{abstract}

%\textbf{World models} have recently attracted significant attention as a framework for learning \textbf{latent representations} that preserve the \textbf{structure and dynamics of the physical world}. Among recent approaches, \textbf{Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA)} are especially compelling because they predict \textbf{masked or future content directly in embedding space}, avoiding costly pixel-level generation while promoting semantic abstraction.

\textbf{World models} aim to capture and predict the \textbf{structure and dynamics of the physical world}, and \textbf{Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA)} are a compelling direction. We study a largely unexplored regime: \textbf{populous, crowded, and chaotic Global South urban environments}, which we call \textbf{DENSEWORLD}, where \textbf{soft spatial boundaries}, \textbf{extreme agent heterogeneity}, \textbf{persistent occlusion}, and rapid \textbf{social negotiation} replace the lane-structured assumptions behind existing evaluations. We release \textbf{DENSEWORLD 1.0}: \textbf{115,687 videos} (\textbf{276 hours}) of drive-through, walk-through, and aerial footage, each 4 to 10 seconds, spanning \textbf{22 Indian cities} and \textbf{714} long-form sources, of which \textbf{86,831} carry automatically derived \emph{layout}, \emph{agent}, and \emph{interaction} targets. \textbf{Ten off-the-shelf frozen encoders} (DINOv2, I-JEPA, LeJEPA, and V-JEPA~1/2.0/2.1) all collapse into a narrow \textbf{37.5\% to 44.4\%} band on an 11-way motion-class probe with a 19.5\% majority baseline, whereas DENSEWORLD-adapted encoders reach \textbf{50.3\% to 53.2\%}.

\textbf{\emph{We introduce FactorJEPA}}, which makes \textbf{\emph{world structure a first-class predictive primitive}}. Rather than adapting a JEPA predictor on undifferentiated video, it performs \textbf{\emph{predictor surgery}}: a three-stage curriculum over automatically separated \textbf{\emph{layout}}, \textbf{\emph{agent}}, and \textbf{\emph{interaction}} views, updating at most $8/48$ (2B) and $6/40$ (1B) encoder blocks with the predictor. Trained on \textbf{86,831} factor-annotated videos and evaluated on \textbf{23,106} held-out samples (1B V-JEPA~2.1), it cuts \textbf{\emph{future-latent L1}} by \textbf{10.6\%}, \textbf{\emph{causal future-block L1}} by \textbf{8.9\%} and \textbf{\emph{mask-ratio slope}} by \textbf{27.4\%} against the frozen backbone, and raises \textbf{\emph{motion-cosine separation}} to \textbf{15.3$\times$} the frozen value, each gain exceeding \textbf{70$\times$} the paired 95\% confidence interval. On both prediction diagnostics it separates in its favour against \textbf{all six} adaptation techniques we evaluate, namely full fine-tuning, linear probing then fine-tuning (LP-FT), low-rank adaptation (LoRA), weight-decomposed low-rank adaptation (DoRA), automatic relative-gradient-norm block selection (Auto-RGN), and vanilla continual self-supervised learning (continual-SSL), at \textbf{both} backbone scales, giving \textbf{24 of 24} paired wins, and beats the strongest full-scale baseline, LoRA, by \textbf{+2.8\%}, \textbf{+1.6\%}, \textbf{+25.2\%} and \textbf{+17.8\%}.

\textbf{20-arm} (2B) and \textbf{14-arm} (1B) studies show rankings \textbf{replicate across backbone scale} at $\rho=0.895$ to $0.978$, establishing the 1B model as a \textbf{half-cost proxy} for 2B selection. On a 10k screening subset, factorization concedes maximally linear motion readout to full fine-tuning ($-44.9\%$ at 2B, $-33.4\%$ at 1B), a trade-off that disappears at full scale, where FactorJEPA leads motion by $+25.2\%$ and gains more than LoRA on \textbf{all four} diagnostics. We release the dataset, checkpoints and the complete 15-diagnostic evaluation harness.





\end{abstract}

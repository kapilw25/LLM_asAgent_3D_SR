\begin{abstract}
\textbf{World models} have recently attracted significant attention as a framework for learning \textbf{latent representations} that preserve the \textbf{structure and dynamics of the physical world}. Among recent approaches, \textbf{Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA)} are especially compelling because they predict \textbf{masked or future content directly in embedding space}, avoiding costly pixel-level generation while promoting semantic abstraction.

In this paper, we study a regime that current world models handle poorly: \textbf{populous, crowded, and chaotic Global South urban environments}, which we call \textbf{DENSEWORLD}. Unlike the cleaner, lower-density, and more structured Western settings that dominate existing evaluations, these scenes are characterized by \textbf{soft spatial boundaries}, \textbf{extreme agent heterogeneity} spanning pedestrians, cars, two-wheelers, carts, animals, delivery vehicles, and rickshaws, \textbf{persistent occlusion}, and rapid \textbf{social negotiation} under mixed traffic. To study this regime, we introduce the \textbf{first large-scale DENSEWORLD dataset} from \textbf{representative dense Global South urban scenes}, comprising \textbf{500 hours of drive-through, walk-through, and aerial video}. Using \textbf{DENSEWORLD}, we show that current \textbf{JEPA} formulations struggle to capture the complexity and interaction structure of \textbf{DENSEWORLD} environments.

\textbf{\emph{We introduce FactorJEPA}}, a \textbf{\emph{factorized JEPA formulation}} that makes \textbf{\emph{world structure a first-class modeling primitive}}. FactorJEPA imposes an explicit \textbf{\emph{additive factorization}} of predictive representations into \textbf{\emph{layout}}, \textbf{\emph{entities}}, and \textbf{\emph{interactions}}, with a \textbf{\emph{soft visibility gate}} for occlusion-aware reasoning. Implemented through \textbf{\emph{learned projections and subspaces}}, this design encourages distinct predictive channels, reducing shortcut mixing across factors and yielding a more interpretable and robust world model. \textbf{\emph{Across DENSEWORLD benchmarks, FactorJEPA outperforms standard JEPA on embedding retrieval, similarity matching, occlusion persistence, and interaction counterfactuals, yielding more stable representations in dense multi-agent scenes.}} To foster future research in this domain, we make the code and evaluation artifacts \href{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/NEPHOS-72D4/README.md}{\textbf{publicly available}}.

\end{abstract}

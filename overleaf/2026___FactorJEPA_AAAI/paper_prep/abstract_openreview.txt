World models aim to capture and predict the structure and dynamics of the physical world, and Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) are a compelling direction. We study a largely unexplored regime: populous, crowded, and chaotic Global South urban environments, which we call DENSEWORLD, where soft spatial boundaries, extreme agent heterogeneity, persistent occlusion, and rapid social negotiation replace the lane-structured assumptions behind existing evaluations. We release DENSEWORLD 1.0: 115,687 videos (276 hours) of drive-through, walk-through, and aerial footage, each 4 to 10 seconds, spanning 22 Indian cities and 714 long-form sources, of which 86,831 carry automatically derived layout, agent, and interaction targets. Ten off-the-shelf frozen encoders (DINOv2, I-JEPA, LeJEPA, and V-JEPA 1/2.0/2.1) all collapse into a narrow 37.5% to 44.4% band on an 11-way motion-class probe with a 19.5% majority baseline, whereas DENSEWORLD-adapted encoders reach 50.3% to 53.2%.

We introduce FactorJEPA, which makes world structure a first-class predictive primitive. Rather than adapting a JEPA predictor on undifferentiated video, it performs predictor surgery: a three-stage curriculum over automatically separated layout, agent, and interaction views, updating at most 8/48 (2B) and 6/40 (1B) encoder blocks with the predictor. Trained on 86,831 factor-annotated videos and evaluated on 23,106 held-out samples (1B V-JEPA 2.1), it cuts future-latent L1 by 10.6%, causal future-block L1 by 8.9% and mask-ratio slope by 27.4% against the frozen backbone, and raises motion-cosine separation to 15.3x the frozen value, each gain exceeding 70x the paired 95% confidence interval. On both prediction diagnostics it separates in its favour against all six adaptation techniques we evaluate, namely full fine-tuning, linear probing then fine-tuning (LP-FT), low-rank adaptation (LoRA), weight-decomposed low-rank adaptation (DoRA), automatic relative-gradient-norm block selection (Auto-RGN), and vanilla continual self-supervised learning (continual-SSL), at both backbone scales, giving 24 of 24 paired wins, and beats the strongest full-scale baseline, LoRA, by +2.8%, +1.6%, +25.2% and +17.8%.

20-arm (2B) and 14-arm (1B) studies show rankings replicate across backbone scale at rho = 0.895 to 0.978, establishing the 1B model as a half-cost proxy for 2B selection. On a 10k screening subset, factorization concedes maximally linear motion readout to full fine-tuning (-44.9% at 2B, -33.4% at 1B), a trade-off that disappears at full scale, where FactorJEPA leads motion by +25.2% and gains more than LoRA on all four diagnostics. We release the dataset, checkpoints and the complete 15-diagnostic evaluation harness.

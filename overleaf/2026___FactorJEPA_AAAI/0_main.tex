\begin{abstractbox}
  \AbstractTitle
  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{spacing}{0.7}

\textbf{Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA)} offer a compelling route to self-supervised world modeling: learn representations by predicting \textbf{masked or future content directly in embedding space}, avoiding pixel-level generation. Yet \textbf{Indic urban scenes are often more chaotic and negotiation-heavy than many Western counterparts}: spatial boundaries are \textbf{soft} and frequently redefined by crowds, many moving objects, and density; agent diversity is \textbf{extreme} with a heavy long tail of actors (people, cars, two-wheelers, carts, animals, delivery vehicles, rickshaws etc.); \textbf{persistent occlusion} induces partial observability as entities vanish and reappear; and interactions are governed by rapid \textbf{social negotiation} rather than rigid rules. These properties expose a central failure mode of current JEPA formulations: predictive embeddings can remain \textbf{implicitly entangled}, enabling shortcuts that blur \textbf{context/layout}, \textbf{entity state}, and \textbf{interaction dynamics}---precisely the structure required for robust urban world models across navigation, robotics, and autonomy.

We introduce \textbf{FactorJEPA}, a JEPA augmentation that makes \textbf{world structure a first-class modeling primitive} through an explicit \textbf{3-factor + 1-gate} predictor. Given a context video clip, FactorJEPA constructs a factor-graph latent state with a \textbf{layout/context} variable $b_t$, \textbf{entity slots} $\{s_t^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{N}$, \textbf{pairwise interaction factors} $\{e_t^{(ij)}\}_{i<j}$, and a \textbf{soft visibility/occlusion gate} $v_t^{(i)}\in(0,1)$ that captures reliability under partial observability.
Crucially, the predictor is constrained to compose the target embedding additively as
\[
\hat y \;=\; \underbrace{A_b b_t}_{\textbf{layout}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\sum_i \underbrace{v_t^{(i)}}_{\substack{\textbf{occlusion/visibility gate}\\ \textbf{soft }(0,1)}}\, A_s s_{t+\Delta}^{(i)}}_{\textbf{entities (visibility-gated)}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\sum_{i<j} A_e e_{t+\Delta}^{(ij)}}_{\textbf{interactions}} \,,
\]
preventing a monolithic predictor from ``hiding'' interaction reasoning and making occlusion explicit by gating only the entity contribution.
To ensure the decomposition is \emph{identifiable in practice}, we impose a second-order \textbf{subspace separation} penalty that suppresses cross-covariance between factor components, and an \textbf{interaction sparsity prior} that learns \textbf{local, interpretable neighborhoods} in dense scenes; visibility is weakly grounded from motion/consistency cues and regularized to avoid trivial always-on gating.
We further couple FactorJEPA with \textbf{motion-aware tube masking} to focus learning on precisely the dynamic, partially observed regions where negotiation and occlusion dominate.

We evaluate FactorJEPA on Indic urban benchmarks (e.g., IDD / IDD-Temporal / IDD-3D) and a suite of targeted stress tests:
\textbf{(i)} transfer to downstream perception (segmentation/detection),
\textbf{(ii)} \textbf{occlusion persistence} and temporal consistency under long-duration partial observability, and
\textbf{(iii)} \textbf{interaction counterfactuals} that probe whether interaction factors change while layout remains invariant.
Across settings, FactorJEPA yields \textbf{more robust predictive representations}, \textbf{improved data efficiency}, and \textbf{markedly better stability} in dense, heterogeneous urban scenes compared to standard JEPA pretraining, while providing \textbf{factor-level diagnostics} (leakage, neighborhood sparsity, visibility fidelity) that correlate with downstream robustness and planning-relevant behavior.



\end{spacing}





%We propose a \textbf{game-theory–inspired} vulnerability metric with a formal \textbf{training$\rightarrow$deployment} asymmetry, and extend the equilibrium definition to admit both \textbf{Nash} and \textbf{Bayesian--Stackelberg} formulations; this yields a \textbf{closed-form threshold certificate} characterizing when models flip from refusal to unsafe compliance under \emph{training-simulation} cues. \textbf{Empirically,} we observe that \textbf{DPO}-style methods reduce cue-driven flipping relative to vanilla \textbf{SFT/BCO}, \textbf{KPO/KTO} further dampens cue sensitivity when kernels encode context invariance, while \textbf{GRPO} exhibits a bimodal pattern depending on whether rewards include \textbf{context-agnostic safety penalties}. \textbf{Larger models} are \emph{more} sensitive to training cues (higher posterior shift), and \textbf{Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)} variants tend to \textbf{amplify} train-simulation effects unless equipped with authenticated oversight or deception penalties. Overall, our results indicate that \textbf{authenticated training signals}, \textbf{penalizing unsafe compliance regardless of purported context}, and \textbf{explicit invariance to unauthenticated ``training'' cues} are critical to driving \textbf{FRACTURE\_FAKING} toward zero.
\end{abstractbox}

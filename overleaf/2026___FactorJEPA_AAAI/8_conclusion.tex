\section{\textbf{Conclusion}}

We studied world modeling in \textbf{populous, crowded, and chaotic Global
South cities}, where heterogeneity, occlusion, and mixed-traffic negotiation
challenge conventional JEPA predictors. We introduced \textbf{DENSEWORLD}:
\textbf{1,000 hours} of drive-through, walk-through, and aerial video collected
across \textbf{22 cities}. Building on this benchmark, we proposed
\textbf{\emph{FactorJEPA}}, which organizes future prediction into explicitly
separated \textbf{\emph{layout}}, \textbf{\emph{agent}}, and
\textbf{\emph{interaction}} channels under soft visibility. Across 2B and 1B
backbones, FactorJEPA delivers \textbf{\emph{(i) more accurate future-latent
prediction}}, \textbf{\emph{(ii) stronger intervention-sensitive structure}},
and \textbf{\emph{(iii) greater robustness under reduced visual evidence}},
with method rankings reproducing across scale at $\rho=0.895$--$0.978$. The
results also expose a consistent trade-off between structured prediction and
maximally linear motion readout. Finally, a
\textbf{Cosmos-initialized latent-to-RGB decoder} renders predicted futures,
while an oracle control separates forecast-induced error from decoder
limitations. Together, these contributions move JEPA world models from
\textbf{\emph{monolithic, silent predictors}} toward
\textbf{\emph{structured and visually inspectable models}} of complex
real-world dynamics.

From Predicting Frames to Explaining Movies: Evaluating Causality, Affordances, Beliefs, and Counterfactuals in World Models


World models are increasingly viewed as a foundation for intelligent agents, robotics, and physical AI. Yet their evaluation remains dominated by future-state prediction, reconstruction quality, and rollout accuracy. While these metrics assess whether a model can anticipate what happens next, they do not determine whether the model understands why events occur, what actions are possible, what agents believe, or how the future would change under alternative interventions. Consequently, it remains unclear whether predictive success is a valid proxy for world understanding.

We address this question through movies, a domain rich in causality, hidden intentions, social interactions, false beliefs, and counterfactual possibilities. Building upon MovieNet, we introduce **CABC-Movie**, a benchmark for evaluating world models along four complementary dimensions of understanding: **Causality** (why events occur), **Affordances** (what actions are available to agents), **Beliefs** (what agents know, do not know, or falsely believe), and **Counterfactuals** (how events would evolve under hypothetical interventions). Starting from MovieNet's aligned movies, scripts, subtitles, character identities, and scene structure, we derive a large-scale evaluation suite consisting of causal explanation tasks, affordance prediction tasks, belief-state tracking tasks, and counterfactual movie-reasoning tasks spanning both short-horizon scenes and long-range narrative dependencies.

We evaluate representative open-source world-model families, including latent predictive world models (**V-JEPA**, **V-JEPA 2**), action-conditioned world models(**JEPA-WM**, **DreamerV3**), and generative world simulators (**Wan 2.1**). To assess whether explanatory information is present in learned world representations, we extract frozen latent trajectories and train lightweight probing heads for CABC prediction, while generative simulators are evaluated through intervention-based rollouts and explanation consistency. We compare these capabilities against conventional prediction metrics including future-state prediction accuracy, latent retrieval performance, event-continuity consistency, and long-horizon rollout quality.

Our analysis reveals a substantial **World Understanding Gap** between future prediction and explanatory reasoning. Models that achieve strong predictive performance often fail to recover latent causes, track character beliefs, or reason about alternative futures. Action-conditioned models demonstrate stronger affordance and counterfactual capabilities, while latent predictive models exhibit superior event continuity and temporal coherence; however, no evaluated world-model family consistently performs well across all four dimensions. These findings suggest that future prediction and world understanding constitute distinct capabilities rather than different manifestations of the same underlying competence.

By deriving CABC-Movie from MovieNet and reframing evaluation from predicting frames to explaining movies, this work provides a new benchmark, taxonomy, and diagnostic framework for assessing whether world models merely forecast future observations or genuinely capture the latent structure of the worlds they seek to model.
